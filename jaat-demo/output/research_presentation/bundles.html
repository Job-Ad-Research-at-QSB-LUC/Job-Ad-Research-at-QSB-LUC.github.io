Extensions of occupational relatedness

From 111,109 OSHA accident and injury narratives, TaskMatch retrieved 6,187 distinct O*NET task IDs. Removing 127 emergency-response task IDs left 6,060 candidates for evidence review. Review with gpt-6-astra retained 610 incident-linked tasks: each had at least one narrative supporting the task’s essential action, object and defining qualifications, and showing its involvement in the incident—performed by the injured worker or by someone whose activity directly exposed or harmed them. We map these tasks to occupations using job postings and compare the resulting indicators with occupational injury and fatality rates.

Incidence versus RCA sensitivity

With the same task bundle and occupation samples, incidence-share scores have higher Spearman correlations than normalized RCA-share scores for overall SOII (0.545 versus 0.398), CFOI (0.515 versus 0.367), and 32 of 33 SOII categories. Explosions/fires is the exception. The task bundle and comparison samples are fixed; this is not held-out model selection.

RCA compares each task’s incidence share within an occupation with its share across all occupations. The tested bundle score is the sum of RCA for bundle tasks divided by the sum of RCA for all retained tasks, pooling specialties before calculation. It is not the occupational-relatedness cosine score.

Methods, sensitivity results and analysis code

Posting incidence shares use occupation-task profiles after the support-five filter and recorded review exclusions; eligible never-reviewed links remain. Distinct-task shares count each retained task once. O*NET scores always use distinct assigned-task shares. Neither score uses RCA or occupational cosine similarity.

SOII measures nonfatal injuries and illnesses. Its R66 table also reports median days and counts in duration bands, including 31 or more days; these are severity-related measures, not fatality rates. Missing rates are not zero. SOII uses exact six-digit codes; CFOI uses disjoint published groups. Rates and profiles describe different populations. These are descriptive associations, not task-specific injury probabilities. Variance accounted for is in-sample R² from ordinary least squares with an intercept and one bundle-share predictor, giving occupations equal weight. Raw-rate and natural-log-rate R² refer to different outcomes; neither is causal or held-out predictive accuracy. Spearman correlation is not R².

What these terms mean
Incident-linked task
An existing O*NET task ID with narrative evidence of performed activity involved in an incident. Membership does not establish how dangerous the task is, or that it caused the injury.
Incident-linked task share
The share of an occupation’s task incidences belonging to the selected bundle. Posting incidences are design-weighted for NLx and unit-weighted for USAJOBS. The distinct-task version counts each task ID once; O*NET uses its assigned task inventory. These measures are not percentages of workers exposed.

Sources, units and comparison methods

CFOI — Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2024 hours-based fatal injury rates, per 100,000 full-time-equivalent workers (FTE). Rates use fatality records and CPS hours worked. We compare 47 disjoint published occupation groups; six have incomplete profile coverage. Source rate table · XLSX · CFOI overview · Rate methods · Data sources · Our mapping and analysis · Coverage sensitivity · CSV.

SOII — Survey of Occupational Injuries and Illnesses. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2023–24 private-industry estimates. Annualized nonfatal DART rates are per 10,000 FTE; DART means days away from work, restricted duty or job transfer. The overall comparison covers 546 six-digit occupations; category samples vary with published data. Nature · R98 · Event · R100 · Duration · R66 · SOII overview and methods · Our analysis.

Measurement. Posting scores divide design-weighted bundle-task incidences by all retained task incidences. O*NET scores divide distinct bundle tasks by all officially assigned tasks. The distinct-task sensitivity uses the same counting rule for both maps. These extend the occupation-task profiles underlying relatedness; they do not test the occupational-relatedness cosine matrix. The posting period (October 2024–September 2025), survey populations and outcome periods differ. Missing or suppressed rates remain unavailable.
