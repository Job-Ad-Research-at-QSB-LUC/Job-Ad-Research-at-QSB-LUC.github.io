Methods: the paired SkillMatch experiment

Sampling, weights, and review

The frame contains non-null posting IDs in each monthly task-extraction table. Skill-only postings absent from that frame cannot enter. The annual occupation target is the smaller of the available count and the larger of 10% (rounded up) or 200. Allocate one observation to each nonempty month, then distribute remaining places proportionally to residual monthly capacity using largest remainders. Deterministic hash draws select postings. A repeated posting in another month is a separate observation. Each observation receives its occupation-month population count divided by its sample count as weight.

The paired sample requires nonempty task and skill IDs before support and review filters. Weights correct sampling rates, not extraction noncoverage. Pre-existing task-review exclusions are applied; unreviewed tasks remain. SkillMatch features do not receive that task-specific LLM review. These are comparisons of full pipelines, not extraction quality alone.

Profiles, occupation coverage, and validation

RCA divides each feature’s weighted incidence share within an occupation by its pooled weighted share across supported construction occupations. Task construction supports 1,012 occupations; SkillMatch supports 1,013. Their intersection with O*NET supports 922 specialties. The comparison pools these into 797 six-digit groups. A benchmark group enters only if all its reference specialties are supported. Benchmark origins/candidate codes are SST 604/671, EMSI 429/697, CPS + SIPP 109/401, and NLSY97 + PSID 77/325, 76/325, and 65/325 at three, five, and ten years. Candidate counts include the origin, which is excluded from its own list.

Group scores average specialty-pair similarities without renormalizing the pooled vectors. Positive scores are ranked after rounding to 12 decimals, breaking ties by destination code. All original outcome mass remains in the denominator, including destinations outside the candidate pool. Survey origins retain the frozen minimum of 40 transitions. Comparisons with O*NET use a tolerance of 10⁻¹². No uncertainty intervals are estimated here.

Advertised requirements are not observed worker competence. Historical transition capture is a descriptive validation of a measure of potential skills-based mobility; it does not estimate hiring probabilities or separate licensing, credentials, geography, wages, and other constraints.

Comparison rule: This representation experiment now uses the same per-origin cutoff as the paper’s comparisons using the same number of recommendations, applied to every model. Another representation: task-linked skills and abilities

All models use the same corrected posting-task data and matched benchmark populations. SST measures the share of recorded transitions captured by the recommended destinations.
